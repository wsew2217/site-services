Purpose of this document

This is the engineering record of how the Reference Deal A global staffing model was built: what data existed at the start, what files the project contains and what each one does, how the AI was taught the methodology it executes, and the case for running solution deals this way. It is written for two audiences: leaders deciding whether AI-assisted solution design should become standard practice, and solution architects who will run the next account through the same pipeline.

Timeline

How it unfolded

June 2026 . The starting point

One ticket extract, nothing else

The customer provided a single extract: roughly 900 sites across 100+ countries with annual ticket counts. No street addresses, no user counts, no ticket-type classification, no second data source of any kind. Historically, mapping an estate of this size takes days of manual lookups and still comes back inaccurate; sizing it is weeks of spreadsheet work that one person can defend and nobody can reproduce.

June 2026 . Sessions 1-3

The methodology transfer and the v1 build

Rather than asking the AI for "a staffing model," the field-services methodology was taught to it as executable rules (detailed below). Out of those working sessions came the complete v1 solution: the virtual campus operating model (28 campuses, 909 sites, 110 technicians, every number derived by rule), two boardroom decks, two reconciled workbooks, a formal methodology document, and a world footprint map. Site geocoding and drive-time clustering, the task that used to take days, completed in an afternoon at higher accuracy than manual mapping ever achieved.

Late June 2026 . The market test

Customer presentation: impressed, and invested

the customer's response to the first pass was the strongest signal a customer can send: they went and pulled the data we told them would sharpen the model, full street addresses and per-site user counts for the entire estate, and returned it as a two-tab extract (DealA_Site_Data_Extract_V2.xlsx).

July 6, 2026 . One working session

The v2 rebuild: full model and all deliverables, same day

The entire model was rebuilt on the new extract in a single session: data reconciliation (which caught duplicate rows inflating the customer's own ticket totals by 12%), a fresh offline geocode of all 989 sites to 97% precision, re-derivation of every campus (all 28 original hubs held; Peru promoted to campus 29 by rule), and regeneration of every deliverable, decks, workbooks, methodology document, map, all reconciling to the new canonical numbers. The same day also produced the derived staffing-ratio analysis (453:1), the site-staffing ladder, the ratio road map to 750:1 and beyond, and the cost-shift and adoption-journey framing.

July 2026 . Packaging

Change summary, executive materials, customer data-request package

The refresh was documented (change summary, executive summary), the 60-site address gap was turned into a fill-in request workbook for the customer, and the build story itself was written up for Dell leadership in Word and PowerPoint form.

The method

How the AI was trained: methodology transfer, not prompting

The critical design decision was made on day one: the AI was not asked to produce a staffing model. It was taught one. Twenty-plus years of field-services judgment was transferred as explicit, executable rules, and the AI's job was to apply them perfectly, at scale, and identically every time. The transfer happened in layers:

Layer 1: The operating concept

The virtual campus: every site belongs to exactly one campus team; each campus is a hub (chosen by demand catchment, not by preference), local sites within drive range, and a remote tail served by queue, dispatch and shipping. Three routed work queues per team so capacity follows demand. Campuses promote and fold automatically as demand moves; nothing is hand-picked.

Layer 2: The sizing arithmetic

Exact throughput rules a technician can actually sustain: about 6 incidents or 4 requests per day on-site, about 5 remote, at 85% utilization on 252 working days; a two-person floor per campus for resilience with a defined single-tech exception; leads 1 per 10 by region; managers 1 per 24. Every number in every deliverable derives from these rules, so every number can be defended.

Layer 3: The judgment no benchmark can see

Where customs borders break a shipping map (Brazil, UK, Canada, Australia, China, Japan stock domestically; Pakistan cannot be served from India). Which countries genuinely can support which (language, time zone, shipping reality). When a large country needs splitting (France into five, India into four, the US into three). When a rolled-up country earns its own campus (Peru, when the data proved Lima's cluster outworked existing hubs). Why geography sets team floors that no users-per-technician benchmark can see.

Layer 4: The narrative discipline

Two voices, never mixed: candid internal framing versus diplomatic customer framing of the same facts. Zero cost content in customer files, enforced by a build flag. The staffing ratio always presented as a derived output, never an assumed benchmark. And a standing quality bar: every file reconciles to the same canonical numbers, verified programmatically before anything ships.

The division of labor: the human supplies judgment and taste; the AI supplies speed, math and perfect consistency. That split is why the output survives customer scrutiny.
The results

What this approach measurably delivered

Speed

v1: complete solution set in working sessions, not project phases. v2: full model rebuild plus every deliverable regenerated and reconciled in one session, same day the data was processed.

Accuracy

989 sites geocoded and drive-time clustered to 97% precision, offline, repeatably. Manual mapping of the same estate historically took days and was never accurate.

Error detection

The pipeline caught duplicate rows in the customer's own extract that overstated their demand by 12%, before the customer knew. We reconciled their data better than they had.

Validation

When better data arrived, all 28 original hub choices re-derived unchanged. The model was confirmed by the data, not rescued from it.

Reproducibility

One command chain rebuilds everything from source. No orphaned spreadsheets, no numbers living only in someone's head, nothing that dies when a person changes roles.

Reusability

A generic engine and intake template reproduce the entire approach for any account. The next solution deal starts at roughly 80% done.

The case for an AI environment in solution deals

This engagement is the proof case for a standing capability, not a one-off experiment. The justification, in the terms that matter to the business:

  • Cycle time is a competitive weapon. Responding to a customer data drop with a fully rebuilt, reconciled solution the same day changes the tempo of a deal. The customer experienced a partner that moves faster than their own data team.
  • One architect, team-scale output. A single senior practitioner produced and maintains nine reconciled deliverables across four formats. The scarce resource, senior judgment, is amplified rather than consumed by production work.
  • Defensibility. Because every number derives from stated rules, any figure in any file can be traced and defended in front of a customer. That is a stronger position than any hand-built spreadsheet has ever offered.
  • Quality control the customer notices. Catching the customer's own data errors, and telling them, converts a vendor relationship into an advisory one.
  • Institutional memory. The methodology now exists as executable rules and a written handoff, not tribal knowledge. It survives staff changes and transfers to the next account by design.
  • Governance fits inside it. The same automation that builds the files enforces the controls: cost segregation between customer and internal variants, reconciliation sweeps, and formatting standards are applied programmatically, every build, without relying on anyone remembering.

The honest boundaries

What the AI did not do: it did not invent the methodology, make the judgment calls, decide what the customer should hear, or own the outcome. Where data was missing it estimated conservatively and flagged every estimate. The approach works because a domain expert directs it; it is a force multiplier for expertise, not a replacement for it.

What comes next

The customer closes the remaining data gaps (60 addresses, ticket-type split) with the request package already prepared. The next major build is the true cost model, transition costs, logistics and depot economics, OEM warranty attach and Life Cycle Hub, turning the ratio road map into a priced business case. The pipeline that exists makes that a build, not a research project.

The methodology is ours. The speed is new. And it is repeatable on every account after this one.
