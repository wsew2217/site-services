How Site Services works inside a customer environment — FMO process flows, role lanes, tech bar / remote overlays, and editable ratio guidance. Same skeleton as Field Services.

What Site Services is

Site Services (and Field Services — same process skeleton) organizes deskside and field work into virtual campuses: one accountable team per site, three routed queues, and overlays for depot, tech bar, and critical coverage. Demand sizes the team; geography chooses the hub; commercial ratios are outputs and guardrails, not day-one drivers.

How we work within an environment

The Future Mode of Operation (FMO) pack describes the operating system inside an estate — not a staffing spreadsheet. Tickets enter from Service Desk, self-service, chat, or walk-up; quality and misroute gates fire; remote-eligible work tries remote first; everything else contacts and schedules into a work tower; logistics (hot swap, ship, receive, quarantine, Smart Hands) feed finalize and asset updates.

Intake→ Misroute / QA→ Remote?→ Contact & schedule→ Tower work→ Logistics→ Finalize

Campus vs remote and partner dispatch are routing overlays, not separate methodologies. Site Services ≡ Field Services on this ladder.

  • Contact ≥ 2× per business day; incidents unreachable after ~3 business days / 3 attempts; requests after ~2 weeks.
  • Escalate after more than 2 misroute transfers; high-priority events prefer voice handoff.
  • Smart Hands: guided short tasks (cap near 60 minutes TOT) — not full break/fix.
  • Quarantine hold default 14 calendar days before reuse / disposition.

Virtual campus anatomy

  • Hub (Staffed) — Anchor site whose catchment maximizes volume inside the drive radius (default ~60 km / ~60 minutes).
  • Local — Sites inside drive range of the hub; same team, on-site reach.
  • Remote — Outside drive range or missing coordinates; remote queue + smart-hands / OEM dispatch and shipping.

Every site belongs to exactly one campus. No orphans. Campuses promote or fold from settings and demand — not hand-picked exceptions as the primary design.

Primary / Secondary / Tertiary role lanes

From deskside operating practice and FS ops packs: every technician has one primary lane. Secondary and tertiary are flex coverage — not additive headcount. Throughput differs sharply by primary lane; do not blend staging/infra with deskside closure rates.

Primary

Home queue and schedule identity — Desktop-Incident, Desktop-Requests, Remote-Desktop, Staging, Site-Lead, Telecom, Network-VDI, Projects.

Secondary

Flex when primary is light — typically Desktop-Requests or peer deskside lane. Used for overflow, not a second FTE.

Tertiary

Surge / specialty cover — often Projects or adjacent tower. Reporting label for ops design.

Tickets assign by role, schedule, location, and active load — not tech self-select. Action-code updates (DSCTM-style) keep workflow coordinators honest on status and availability.

Tech bar, virtual tech bar & remote support

  • On-site tech bar — Walk-up incidents/requests, refresh/peripheral handoff, new-hire onboarding help, visible IT presence (deskside delivery model).
  • Virtual tech / remote desktop — Resolve before dispatch when eligible; dedicated virtual technicians can cover named remote metros from a home office (see Deal B).
  • Remote resolution team — Advanced remote support for deskside/mobility that L1 cannot close; shift-left keeps deskside for break/fix, IMACD, and projects.
  • Staging / depot — Campus staging within ~25 mi; centralized depot for remote sites + remote support tech; inventory lifecycle, hot swap, RMA, refresh.
  • Smart Hands / partner dispatch — Physical touch when not local; complements remote software support.

Staffing math

  • Working days: 252 · Utilization: ~85% · Team floor: typically 2
  • On-site: ~6 incidents / tech / day, ~4 requests / tech / day
  • Remote: ~5 tickets / tech / day
  • Vac/sick, travel utilization, SLA mix, and 24×7 floors are settings overlays
