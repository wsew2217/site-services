Site Services · Generic

Structure & methodology

The operating model for any estate — not one account’s campus list. Same process flows as Field Services.

What Site Services is

Site Services (and Field Services — same process skeleton) organizes deskside and field work into virtual campuses: one accountable team per site, three routed queues, and overlays for depot, tech bar, and critical coverage. Demand sizes the team; geography chooses the hub; commercial ratios are outputs and guardrails, not day-one drivers.

Virtual campus anatomy

  • Hub (Staffed) — Anchor site whose catchment maximizes volume inside the drive radius (default ~60 km / ~60 minutes).
  • Local — Sites inside drive range of the hub; same team, on-site reach.
  • Remote — Outside drive range or missing coordinates; remote queue + smart-hands / OEM dispatch and shipping.

Every site belongs to exactly one campus. No orphans. Campuses promote or fold from settings and demand — not hand-picked exceptions as the primary design.

Demand when data is sparse

  1. Annual tickets if present
  2. Else users/seats × estate ticket rate
  3. Else devices × device ticket rate
  4. Else placeholder with a low-confidence flag

Without coordinates, a site cannot be auto-Local — it stays Remote / Uncertain.

Staffing math

  • Working days: 252 · Utilization: ~85% · Team floor: typically 2
  • On-site: ~6 incidents / tech / day, ~4 requests / tech / day
  • Remote: ~5 tickets / tech / day
  • Vac/sick, travel utilization, SLA mix, and 24×7 floors are settings overlays (structure from legacy PFS; rates are modern placeholders)
  • Primary / Secondary / Tertiary role lanes are reporting splits of sized headcount — secondary/tertiary are flex, not additive HC

Overlays

Depot / staging, tech bars, VIP, virtual techs, critical 24×7, partner-remote share. Coverage classes: dedicated business hours, local drive-range, dispatch/remote, critical 24×7.

Cost & scenarios

Day-one cost follows throughput-sized staffing plus dispatch, shipment, depot, stock, refresh, and transition cash. Ratio rungs (750:1 → 1500:1) are a commercial road map and cost-shift story — not the engine that invents campus count. Use the generic cost model for any estate; Reference Deal A has its own deal-specific cost model.

Process flows

Intake → misroute check → contact / schedule → tower work → logistics → finalize. Site Services and Field Services share this ladder (FMO process packs). Action codes, auto-assign by role/location/load, and ops KPIs (DOH, aging, staffing gap, SLO) sit on top for design QA and live control.
