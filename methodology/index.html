Site Services and Field Services

Site Services and Field Services share the same process-flow model (FMO campus/remote break-fix, IMAC, Smart Hands, quarantine, staging, partner dispatch). Labels differ commercially; the operating skeleton does not: intake → misroute → contact/schedule → tower work → logistics → finalize.

Primary / Secondary / Tertiary role lanes

Every technician has one primary lane. Secondary and tertiary are flex coverage — not additive headcount. The deal engine sizes field techs from throughput, then reports a Primary HC mix (Desktop-Incident, Desktop-Requests, Remote-Desktop, Staging, Site-Lead, Telecom, Network-VDI, Projects) for schedule and ops design. Throughput differs sharply by primary lane; do not blend staging/infra with deskside closure rates.

The model in one paragraph

The estate is organized into virtual campuses: every one of the 989 sites belongs to exactly one campus team. Each campus is a hub (the anchor site whose 60 km catchment captures the most demand), local sites within drive range served on-site, and a remote tail served through a remote queue with OEM or vetted smart-hands dispatch and shipping. Each team runs three routed queues, campus, local and remote, and technicians flex across them, so capacity follows demand rather than location. Campuses promote and fold automatically as demand moves; nothing in the structure is hand-picked.

Geographic and structural rules

  • Country splits by nearest hub: France 5 campuses (Paris, Lyon, Nantes, Marseille, Lille), India 4 (Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Kolkata, Bangalore), USA 3 by longitude (West / Central / East).
  • Rollups: 84 low-volume countries roll into a serving campus, paired by customs, language, time zone and shipping reality (Bangladesh to India-Kolkata; Pakistan to UAE because India cannot ship across that border; Mexico, the Caribbean and Central America to Colombia; the Nordics and Iceland to Germany; Sri Lanka to India-Bangalore).
  • Customs-locked countries stock and staff domestically: Brazil, UK, Canada, Australia, China, Japan.
  • Customer-staffed exception: Belgium, contractual on-site presence at extreme-low volume.
  • Hub vs depot role: hub-led when at least half the campus workload is on-site; depot-led (remote and shipping led) otherwise. v2: 11 hubs, 18 depots.
  • Promotion rule: a rolled country earns its own campus when its on-site-able 60 km cluster outworks the smallest organic own-campus, especially across a customs border. Applied to Peru in v2.

The staffing ratio, and why it is what it is

At 114 field technicians against 51,680 users the model implies one technician per 453 users, with each campus's full remote tail included. The ratio is an output, standing on: (a) call volume projections, 87,163 tickets a year converted to daily rates per site; (b) the distribution of sites, 989 locations across 104 countries, which sets minimum team sizes by geography; (c) estimated time on task at deliberately conservative rates; and (d) conservative allowances where per-site time-on-task and ticket-type classification are not yet available.

The road map (campus geographic minimums kept, each step unlocked by adoption data, re-derived by rule): about 90 technicians at 750:1, the proposed long-term goal; 74 at the 1,000:1 stretch horizon; 67 and 63 at the 1,200 to 1,500:1 art-of-the-possible end.

The trade behind it: each rung shifts cost rather than simply cutting labor. Shipping and courier volume, depot throughput, smart-hands dispatch and OEM on-site warranty usage rise, with Dell's OEM warranty model the strongest expression of moving break-fix onto the manufacturer. The offsets are remote-first resolution and consolidated stock management with real asset visibility. The end state: Intune fully realized with Autopilot, combined with Dell's global Life Cycle Hub, devices ship factory-configured from off-site depots directly to the user, and a new install or refresh becomes a shipping event requiring no technician.
